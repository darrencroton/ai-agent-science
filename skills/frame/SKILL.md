# frame

How an idea becomes a question that could be answered, and what answering it would need. It needs no paper and no workspace. Paths are relative to the installation root. Read `skills/_shared/core.md` whole first; it holds here, above all rule 4 (a claim no stronger than its test), rule 5 (the framing is a proposal, and its owner decides) and rule 6 (read what is there; run nothing).

## What it is given

- The idea or question in its owner's words, and why it matters to them.
- The material to hand (results, plots, notes, code), each at a version, to be read and not run. There may be none: everything under item 2 is then open, and there is nothing for a blind read to read.
- What the framing is for: starting an investigation, choosing between questions, beginning a paper.
- Where the result goes and who decides. With no place named, it is given in the conversation and nothing is written.

## What it returns

About a page, in this order, with the search record and the declared test after it.

1. **The claim, as one sentence that could be tested.** If it cannot be written yet, that is the first finding. Say what is claimed, about what, and by what method: a paper can already have done any one of the three.
2. **What it rests on now.** For each part of the claim, the material that supports it, cited by path and place, and what is missing. A part that rests on nothing is an open question, never a guess.
3. **What the literature says.** The `literature` skill (`skills/literature/SKILL.md`): its novelty and build-on questions, and for the claim itself what supports and what contradicts it. Give the closest work first, what it left open in its authors' own words, what was searched, and which papers were read in full and which only as abstracts.
4. **Two or three framings.** Each is a short paragraph: what it puts first, what it gains, what it costs and what it would need. Say which you would choose and why. Where the framing may be in the framer's head and not in the material, commission a **blind framing read**: a fresh context given the material and no framing, asked what it would claim and what a referee would say.
5. **The declared test** for each open part (below).
6. **Questions for the owner**, each with a recommendation.

## The declared test

A claim with nothing under it becomes a question with a declared test, written before anything is run, for its owner or for an investigation run elsewhere (a campaign). The question states the claim as a question, the headline result that would answer it and what would count against it; what the literature already says; and what its kind of test needs. For a measurement that is what is measured, on what, and what is held fixed; the effective size of the design; each threshold with its reason; and the holdout, named before the first change. A derivation, a check of what a data release covers or a question of provenance has its own equivalents: choose what applies. A number in the question that is recalled and not yet sourced is labelled so (core rule 1).

Before the question goes out, the challenger reads it (`skills/readers/challenger.md`): its attacks are the checklist, and what it is asked is whether the test as declared could tell the claimed outcome from its alternatives. Where much will be spent on the answer, a referee's angle on the result as it would be worded shows what a journal would ask of it (`skills/readers/referee.md`).
