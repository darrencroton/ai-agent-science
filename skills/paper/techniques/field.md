# The field

How to build `paper/field.md`, what papers of this kind contain and what the field accepts, so that the outline is set, and the paper shaped and refereed, against that and not against a generic ideal. Read it at start, once the paper's field and genre (a methods paper, a survey, a software paper) are clear; or at the first need of the stylist or the referee.

## Reading the field

Commission it to a fresh context, so that the norms are not chosen to suit the draft. Give it only the paper's title, a paragraph saying what the paper does, its field and genre, and any exemplars already in `paper/lit/index.md`. It uses the `literature` skill (or the field's own index where ADS does not serve it, recording any limit on coverage) and reads recent papers closest in kind and in subject, from the target journal (if one is chosen) and the field's leading journals, until new papers stop adding norms. It says so and gives the count: about ten is typical, never fewer than six.

## What to record

The papers read, each with its key and why it was chosen. Then their form: each one's headings in order, length, figure and table counts and what the figures do; the template they share and where they diverge. What this paper has that the template has no slot for, with the precedent for where it goes, is returned to the session for the outline and not written into the file, which describes the field only; that finding is usually the one that matters.

Then the norms, each with the papers that show it and where:

- what such a paper must contain, and at what level of detail;
- the standard benchmarks and the agreement accepted, in the source's own units and conventions (core rule 1);
- validation, convergence and testing practice;
- how uncertainty and significance are shown;
- code and data availability;
- what counts as new and what as textbook.

Where practice varies, give a range and not a norm; a norm seen in one paper only is marked as such.

Two or three papers of the same genre are the exemplars: they give the stylist its standard of narrative (`skills/paper/readers/stylist.md`) and are the first papers of the referee's reading. For each, record its story line, quoted with locations: what its first paragraph and the introduction's last paragraph do, the order in which it introduces its ideas and why that order serves the reader, how its sections hand on, and how it closes. Each gets a row in `paper/lit/index.md` saying what it is an analogue of.

## Returning it

The session confirms a sample of the norms at source before committing the file (core rule 3), and proposes any structural change to `paper/outline.md` for the author. The file is agent-written and never cited. It gains a part, added and not rewritten, when the paper's focus adds a field or a genre.
